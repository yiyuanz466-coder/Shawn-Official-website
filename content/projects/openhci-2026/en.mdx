## Context

I mentored Group 6 as a teaching assistant at the **OpenHCI 2026 workshop**, whose theme was "identity, agency and responsibility after AI intervenes."

With social media woven into daily life, our idea of a "good" photo is shaped by trending compositions, filters, poses and algorithms, and personal taste can quietly give way to mainstream style.

## Problem

The team framed the problem through three papers:

- **Ideas need a medium to take shape**: inner ideas start out vague and are gradually formed through external media (Suwa & Tversky, 1997). Photography is the same: between the idea and the photo, people often get stuck not knowing how to shoot.
- **Social media pre-shapes our imagination**: idealized images on social media shape what people expect a photo to look like, steering where they shoot, how they pose and how they frame (Siegel et al., 2022). Most travel photos fall into a handful of common Instagram angles.
- **AI should help people express, not hand out answers**: AI should help users turn vague feelings and ideas into visible, adjustable expression (Brade et al., 2023).

<Callout title="Research question">
How can AI help users turn inner feelings into concrete images while preserving their own photographic judgment and agency?
</Callout>

## Research & insights

The team combined a literature review, **qualitative interviews** and **prototype testing**, analyzing decisions such as editing prompts, reshooting, and adopting or departing from AI suggestions.

- **Participants**: 6 people (2 men, 4 women), aged 20–25, university students and new graduates
- **Criteria**: all take travel photos, have used AI tools, and shoot mainly on phones

<Figure src="images/journey-as-is.jpg" alt="Current user journey: find a moment worth capturing, imagine the ideal photo, start shooting, review and save; the emotion curve dips during shooting and review" caption="Current journey: emotion bottoms out while shooting and reviewing. “Am I doing this right?” “It doesn't look like what I imagined…”" wide />

<Callout title="Key insight">
Users don't lack examples; they **don't know how to turn the feeling in their head into a concrete pose and composition**. An AI that hands them a standard answer pulls them further from the photo they wanted.
</Callout>

## Ideation

Rather than offering trending photos to imitate, SoulSee first guides users to describe what they want to express:

- What am I feeling right now?
- How do I want to present myself?
- What mood or atmosphere should the photo carry?

AI then translates these feelings into **pose, expression and composition suggestions**, so every shot starts from the user's own intent. The core idea: **the AI steps back, the judgment stays**.

<Figure src="images/concept.jpg" alt="Three design concepts: a green photo-suggestion ghost, an AI coach that gradually steps back, and a GIF recording decisions before the shutter" caption="Three concepts: a suggestion ghost, a fading AI coach, and a record of pre-shutter decisions." wide />

- **Suggestion ghost**: turns abstract emotions into poses, expressions and compositions users can mirror, easing the "I don't know how to shoot this" problem.
- **An AI coach that fades out**: as users gain experience, hints drop from three to two (random), to one, to none.
- **Pre-shutter decision record**: a GIF captures how pose, expression and framing were adjusted, revealing how a personal style forms.

## Design & iteration

The team built the concept into a web photo booth, **(AI)dentity**, with five steps:

1. **Choose a format**: one, two or four frames; the format sets the aspect ratio.
2. **Generate a ghost**: describe a style in a prompt to generate a "ghost" preview.
3. **Shoot**: a translucent ghost overlays the camera; align your pose with an opacity slider, pick a filter, press the shutter.
4. **Result**: compare the shots and download the strip, a QR code or a GIF, or reshoot.
5. **Album**: photos are collected into an album, filterable by ghost, outline or no-assist mode.

## Solution

With SoulSee, the journey gains two new stages: "ask the AI for help" and "adjust and reshoot." Users can accept, modify or reject each suggestion, and a final recap lets them see how they have grown.

## Reflection

This was my first time switching from participant to TA. The year before, I joined OpenHCI myself (with DearPlanet); this year I walked another team from literature and interviews all the way to a working prototype.

Their topic stayed with me: as AI gets better at giving suggestions, designers need to think harder about **when the AI should step back**. Good AI assistance should leave people more capable, not more dependent.
